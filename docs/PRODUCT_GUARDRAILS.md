# Momma D's Garden Product Guardrails

This app is for one primary person first: Momma D. It should feel calm, useful, warm, and impressive without becoming a dashboard jungle.

## North Star

Help Momma D know what to do in her garden, when to do it, and why it matters, using simple guidance backed by strong horticultural science.

The app should make her feel:

- confident about today's garden decisions
- proud of what she is growing
- curious without being overwhelmed
- excited to show friends and family

## Primary user

Momma D is the default user. Design for her first.

Assumptions:

- She may use the app mostly on a phone.
- She may occasionally use a tablet.
- She should not need to understand databases, accounts, or technical setup.
- She should not be forced to manage a complex system just to get value.
- She likely wants clear next steps more than raw botanical data.

## Product personality

The app should feel like:

- a friendly garden coach
- a tidy notebook
- a calm seasonal guide
- a smart assistant that explains the why only when useful

It should not feel like:

- enterprise software
- a spreadsheet with flowers painted on it
- a social network
- a research database
- a chores app that nags constantly

## Core rule: one useful thing at a time

Every screen should answer one of these questions:

1. What should I do today?
2. What is growing where?
3. Is anything at risk?
4. What should I plant or move next?
5. Why does this recommendation matter?

If a feature does not answer one of those questions, it probably belongs later or not at all.

## Feature restraint rules

Before adding a feature, ask:

- Will Momma D use this in the garden with dirty hands?
- Can the value be understood in under 10 seconds?
- Does it reduce uncertainty or add clutter?
- Can it be hidden behind a simple summary?
- Does it work on a phone first?

Avoid adding:

- too many tabs
- dense charts
- abstract analytics
- multi-step configuration
- social/community features
- advanced settings before the core loop works

## The core loop

The app should center on a repeatable loop:

1. Open app.
2. See today's garden brief.
3. Check urgent tasks or watch-outs.
4. View plants/zones only if needed.
5. Log a quick note or photo.
6. Leave the app and enjoy the garden.

The user should not need to browse eight areas to understand the day.

## Information hierarchy

Always show information in this order:

1. Action: what to do.
2. Reason: why it matters.
3. Timing: when to do it.
4. Context: which plant or garden zone.
5. Science: deeper explanation, collapsed or secondary.

Botanical depth should be available, not unavoidable.

## Mobile-first principles

- One primary action per section.
- Large tap targets.
- Short cards.
- Plain language labels.
- Avoid tiny tab labels where possible.
- Prioritize vertical scrolling over complex grids.
- Assume the user is outside in sunlight.
- Avoid low-contrast decorative text.

Tablet layout can add side-by-side views, but phone should never be a compromised version.

## Garden intelligence principles

The app can be smart, but it must be humble.

Good intelligence:

- “Water patio pots this evening because containers dry fast after hot days.”
- “Train cucumber vines onto the trellis this week while they are flexible.”
- “Do not plant mint in the ground. Keep it in a pot.”

Bad intelligence:

- “Optimize evapotranspiration model.”
- “Nutrient plan variance: 17%.”
- “Configure rule engine thresholds.”

Use expert science to create simple guidance.

## Backyard model

The app should organize Momma D's garden around real physical spaces:

- Patio pots / containers
- Raised bed with trellis
- In-ground garden bed
- Perimeter flowers and pollinator border
- Optional future zones: compost, herbs, seed-starting, berry patch

A plant should usually belong to a zone. A task should usually belong to a plant, zone, or season.

## First-season scope

The first useful version should support:

- Garden brief
- Garden zones
- Plant list
- Placement recommendations
- Weekly tasks
- Simple garden log
- Photo/note capture concept
- Demo guide

Save for later:

- accounts/auth
- family collaboration
- complex inventory
- marketplace/service providers
- heavy analytics
- full AI automation
- paid product flows

## Definition of done for new features

A feature is not done until:

- It works on phone width.
- It has a simple empty/demo state.
- It has plain-language copy.
- It avoids unnecessary setup.
- It supports the core garden loop.
- It does not add a new major navigation area unless truly necessary.

## Product mantra

Useful first. Beautiful second. Clever third. Complicated never.
